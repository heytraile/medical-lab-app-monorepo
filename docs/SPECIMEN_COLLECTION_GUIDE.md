# Specimen collection guide (plain English)

**Who this is for:** Anyone working at the front desk, accession bench, or draw station who is **not** a doctor or nurse — reception, phlebotomy trainees, IT, admin.

**Why it exists:** Different tests need different **physical samples** (specimens). If you put the wrong test on the wrong tube, the lab cannot run it — or the result is wrong. Our accession app splits orders by specimen type so **each tube gets its own accession number and label**.

---

## The big idea in one sentence

**A specimen is whatever you actually collect from the patient** (blood in a tube, urine in a cup, etc.) **so the lab can run the ordered tests.**

---

## Quick reference

| Specimen | Plain English | Usually drawn as | Common tube / container | In our catalog? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Whole blood** | Blood with all cells and liquid still mixed | Needle → blood flows into tube | Lavender (purple), light blue, red (depends on test) | Yes — `blood` |

| **Urine** | Pee | Patient urinates into cup (or 24 h jug) | Sterile urine cup / 24 h container | Yes — `urine` |

| **Other** | Swabs, fluid, tissue, etc. | Varies by test | Swab kit, sterile jar | In app as `other` |

## Whole blood (`blood`)

### What is it?

Blood **as it comes out of the vein**, with **red cells, white cells, platelets, and plasma** all still together. Nothing has been spun or separated yet.

### What is it used for?

Tests that need to **count or look at the cells**, or need **whole blood chemistry**:

- **CBC** (complete blood count) — how many red/white cells, platelets, etc.
- **ESR**, **Hb / PCV**, **platelets**, **PT/INR**, **PTT** — bleeding/clotting and blood disorders
- **Blood type**, **cross-match** — transfusion work
- Many **haematology** tests in our catalog

### How do you collect it?

1. Identify the patient (name, DOB, order).
2. Wash hands, gloves, tourniquet above the draw site.
3. Clean the skin (alcohol), let it dry.
4. Insert needle, attach tube(s) that the order requires.
5. Fill tube to the line, **mix gently** if the tube requires it (lavender tubes for CBC must be inverted 8–10 times — don’t shake hard).
6. Release tourniquet, remove needle, pressure + bandage.
7. Label tube **at the bedside** with patient info / use printed label from accession.

### Tube colors (typical — always follow your lab’s SOP)

| Color | Additive | Often used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lavender / purple** | EDTA | CBC, blood smears |
| **Light blue** | Citrate | Coagulation (PT, PTT) |
| **Red** (no gel) | None | Some chemistry if lab accepts whole blood |
| **Green** | Heparin | Some chemistry / blood gas (site-specific) |

### Common mistakes

- Under-filling a tube (especially light blue for coag).
- Not mixing EDTA tube → CBC can be wrong.
- Using serum tube when the order needs **whole blood** (CBC on a gold top = problem).

## Serum (`serum`)

### What is it? (Read this if you’ve ever asked “what the fuck is serum?”)

**Serum is the yellowish liquid part of blood *after* the blood has clotted and the solid clot has been removed.**

Think of it like this:

1. You draw blood into a tube.
2. The blood **clots** (thickens) in the tube.
3. The tube goes in a **centrifuge** (spin machine).
4. The heavy cells and clot pack at the bottom.
5. The **clear/yellow liquid on top** = **serum**.

**Serum is NOT the same as whole blood.**  
**Serum is NOT the same as plasma** (plasma is the liquid *before* clotting — used for some tests, but our catalog mostly says “serum” for chemistry/immunology).

What’s *in* serum? Water, salts, proteins, sugar, cholesterol, enzymes, hormones, antibodies — all the stuff chem and immuno machines measure **without** needing intact blood cells.

### What is it used for?

Most **blood chemistry**, **hormones**, and **immunology** in our catalog, for example:

- **Glucose**, **creatinine**, **electrolytes**, **liver enzymes** (ALT, AST), **lipids/cholesterol**
- **TSH**, **PSA**, **pregnancy blood tests** (BHCG)
- **HIV**, **hepatitis**, **RA**, **CRP**, and many other **antibody** tests

### How do you collect it?

1. Same vein draw as blood — patient experience is identical (“they’re taking my blood”).
2. Use the tube type your lab specifies for serum — usually **gold/tiger top (SST)** or **plain red**, which may contain clot activator and gel to separate serum after spin.
3. Fill to the line, invert gently if required (follow tube insert — often 5 inversions for SST).
4. Let clot **if** your SOP requires bench time before spin (many SST tubes clot on the way to lab).
5. **Centrifuge** within the lab’s time window (often within 1–2 hours — lab policy wins).
6. Label and send; accession as **serum** tube — separate accession from CBC if both ordered.

### Why is serum a separate tube from whole blood?

Machines that run **CBC** need **cells intact in EDTA**.  
Machines that run **chemistry** need **cell-free liquid** (serum).  
If you mix them on one tube type, **one side of the order loses**.

That’s why our app gives **CBC + BMP** → **two tubes, two accession numbers**.

### Common mistakes

- Running chemistry on lavender EDTA blood (many chem analyzers reject it).
- Hemolysis (broken red cells) from rough handling → can skew potassium, LDH, etc.
- Delaying centrifuge too long on some orders.
- Fasting: lipids and glucose fasting tests need patient fasting — order says so, not the tube color.

## Urine (`urine`)

### What is it?

**Urine** — fluid filtered by the kidneys. Collected from the patient urinating into a container, not from a needle (except rare specialized setups).

### What is it used for?

- **Urinalysis (UA)** — infection, blood in urine, sugar, protein, etc.
- **Pregnancy test (urine)** — BHCG in urine
- **24-hour** collections — total protein, creatinine clearance, some hormones/metabolites
- **Microalbumin** — kidney damage screening (often diabetic care)

### How do you collect it?

**Random / clean-catch (most common for UA):**

1. Give patient a **clean cup** and instructions.
2. **Clean-catch:** clean genital area, start urinating, then mid-stream into cup (first splash often has skin bacteria).
3. Pour into labeled container if needed; cap tightly.
4. Label with patient info; note **collection time** on the order / specimen info screen.

**24-hour urine:**

1. Patient gets a **large jug**, often with preservative.
2. **First morning void:** patient urinates and **discards** that one.
3. **Then** every urine for the next 24 hours goes **into the jug**.
4. **Last** sample: next morning’s first void **goes in** the jug.
5. Total volume recorded; lab may need aliquot — follow SOP.

### Common mistakes

- Unlabeled cup (never acceptable).
- Bacterial contamination from non clean-catch when culture is ordered.
- Wrong 24 h start/stop (discarding the wrong void).
- Leaving cup at room temperature too long for some tests.

## Stool (`stool`)

### What is it?

**Feces / poop** — used for digestive, infection, and occult blood testing.

### What is it used for? (general lab — not all in our catalog yet)

- **Occult blood** — hidden blood in stool (colon cancer screening)
- **Ova & parasites**
- **C. diff**, **culture** for certain diarrheal illness
- **Fecal immunochemical test (FIT)**

### How do you collect it?

1. Patient uses a **hat** in toilet or special paper.
2. Transfer **small amount** with spoon into **stool container** (never overfill).
3. Cap tightly; label with date/time.
4. Some tests need **refrigeration** or rapid delivery — check order.

### Common mistakes

- Mixing with urine or toilet water.
- Too much stool in cup (lab needs a small sample).
- Wrong preservative kit for the test ordered.

## Other specimens (`other`)

Catch-all for things that are **not** standard blood/urine/stool:

| Type | Examples | How collected (typical) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Throat / nasal swab** | Strep, flu, COVID | Swab back of throat or nose; put in transport tube |
| **Wound swab** | Infection culture | Swab affected area; avoid normal skin |
| **Sputum** | TB, respiratory culture | Deep cough into sterile cup (morning sample often preferred) |
| **CSF** | Meningitis workup | **Doctor** collects via lumbar puncture — not front desk |
| **Tissue / biopsy** | Pathology | **Clinic/OR** — formalin jar, labeled by site |
| **Saliva** | Some DNA or drug screens | Spit into tube to line |

Our app uses `other` when we add tests that don’t fit blood/serum/urine/stool buckets.

## How this maps to our accession app

When you select tests on the **Accession** page:

1. The app reads each test’s **specimen hint** from the catalog (`blood`, `serum`, `urine`, …).
2. Tests that share the same hint go on **one tube** → **one accession number** → **one label**.
3. Different hints → **separate tubes**, **separate accessions**, **separate labels** (scrollable preview + grid view).

**Example order:** CBC + creatinine + urinalysis

| Tube | Specimen | Tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Whole blood | CBC |
| 2 | Serum | Creatinine (and other chemistry on that tube) |
| 3 | Urine | Urinalysis |

Three labels. Three accessions. One patient. One requisition.

## Words you’ll hear (mini glossary)

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Phlebotomy** | The act of drawing blood from a vein |
| **Venipuncture** | Needle stick into a vein |
| **Centrifuge** | Machine that spins tubes to separate liquid from cells/clot |
| **Aliquot** | A portion split from a sample into another container |
| **Hemolysis** | Red blood cells broke open — can ruin some results |
| **Fasting** | Patient ate nothing (usually 8–12 h) — needed for some glucose/lipid tests |
| **Clean-catch** | Mid-stream urine technique to reduce contamination |
| **SST** | Serum separator tube — gold “tiger top” with gel after spin |
| **EDTA** | Anticoagulant in lavender tubes — keeps blood liquid for cell counts |

## Disclaimer

This guide is **education for staff**, not medical advice. **Tube types, draw order, volumes, and timing** must follow **Drax Hall / your lab’s written SOP** and the **test directory** on each order. When in doubt, ask the lab supervisor or pathologist.
